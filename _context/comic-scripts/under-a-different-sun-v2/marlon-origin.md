# Under a Different Sun

## Visual direction

Two portrait-format pages, each exactly four panels in a 2×2 grid. Dramatic idealized superhero comic: bold black brush shapes, angular expressive drawing, heroic silhouettes, clear flat colors and limited cel shadows. Use the existing graphic character sheets unchanged. This is one Marlon at two ages, not two characters. Panels 1–4: younger, bald, dark short beard, black T-shirt and black jeans. Panels 5–8: older, bald, visibly speckled silver-gray beard; civilian black T-shirt/jeans on arrival, then the sheet's black superhero suit and cape, never a cowl or mask. The age shift is fictional time compression. Red pencil is the visual form of pattern recognition and connections. Before escape: a red-sun writing-program planet. After escape: the warm funded yellow sun of technology, media, and academic projects. Preserve the character designs; create only story images. Render every Caption as a distinct pale-yellow RECTANGULAR NARRATION BOX with clear dark lettering; dialogue goes in white speech balloons with tails pointing to the correct speaker. Allow sufficient space for full sentences. Render only explicitly quoted Caption, Dialogue, and Lettering text. Books and background documents have unlettered marks, never extra slogans. Fictional building design, not documentary architecture. The latest correction overrides the older brief: no claim he was hired for video, no camera as an escape ticket, no video-reel comparison.

## Panel 1 — The writing planet

Art: Wide view inside a seminar room on a rocky alien planet beneath an enormous red sun. Younger Marlon and several differently drawn writing teachers around a table full of drafts. Everyone casually supports and rearranges parts of a magnificent floating structure of manuscript pages and conceptual diagrams. Marlon is one among capable colleagues, not yet exceptional. The landscape beyond the glass is unmistakably another planet.

Caption: “Marlon came from Expos, Harvard's writing program.”

Caption: “On that planet, they taught writing. They studied how ideas fit together.”

## Panel 2 — Ordinary powers

Art: Closer view of younger Marlon working alongside a colleague and a visiting researcher. Several floating clusters contain distinct unlettered visual systems: a mathematical diagram, a historical map, and a literary manuscript. He notices the shared structure and draws a red line joining two ideas; the researcher begins rearranging their own pages into a coherent whole. Show actual pattern recognition across academic work, not grading errors or correcting punctuation. His face, pencil gesture, and the coherent arrangement must read clearly.

Caption: “They could enter an unfamiliar academic world, spot its patterns, and help someone turn a tangle of notes into work that made sense.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “These two ideas belong together.”

## Panel 3 — The ground moves

Art: The same writing seminar tilts as cracks spread through the ground outside. Teachers keep working together with levitating pages; one hand steadies a teacup. A retro rocket is visible at the distant landing platform. Camp cosmic peril, not actual casualties. The world is threatened, not conclusively destroyed.

Caption: “To its inhabitants, none of this was a superpower. It was the work.”

Caption: “But the ground beneath that world was never quite secure.”

## Panel 4 — What he carried

Art: Younger Marlon strapped into a departing rocket, surrounded by annotated drafts, notebooks, and books. He still studies and connects pages with his red pencil as stars stretch beyond the window. In the distance a brilliant red flare obscures the home planet; its survival remains possible. No camera prop, hiring scene, ticket, rescuer, or asserted reason for departure. Luggage emphasizes intellectual habits carried into a new world.

Caption: “When Marlon left, he took the habits of that world with him.”

## Panel 5 — A funded sun

Art: Older Marlon, recognizable silver-gray beard, climbs from the same rocket in his black T-shirt and jeans. The Bok Center stands under a huge yellow sun. Welcoming collaborators carry manuscripts and equipment; code screens, cameras, and academic diagrams coexist. No camera handoff, recruitment speech, or special focus on why he was hired. The yellow light illuminates abundant working materials.

Caption: “In 2010, he landed at the Bok Center, in a world of code, cameras, and academic projects.”

Caption: “This world had a different sun: funding.”

Lettering: Building name: “BOK CENTER”.

## Panel 6 — A familiar problem

Art: A collaborator stands with a mass of research notes, diagrams and possible images floating over a worktable. A perfectly functional camera and a computer stand nearby. Older Marlon, now in his black superhero suit and cape, studies the material with the same attentive head tilt and pencil gesture as panel 2. The collaborator is intellectually engaged, not helpless or incompetent.

Caption: “The tools were different. The questions were familiar.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “Let's find the structure.”

## Panel 7 — Under a different sun

Art: Older Marlon's red pencil connects the floating ideas. They unfold into a legible structure of argument, pictures and code-like symbols; collaborators actively take pieces and develop their own projects. Strong graphic echo of panel 2: same ability, changed context. Marlon is surprised by their surprise, not smug. Caped figure and the red connecting line provide the heroic scale.

Caption: “Here, his old abilities became superpowers: recognizing patterns, learning new systems, and helping other people's ideas take shape.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “We did this all the time back home.”

## Panel 8 — Room for a team

Art: Wide view into a bustling, colorful collaborative hall visibly INSIDE the Bok Center. An outer arch says Bok Center, an inner doorway says Learning Lab. Several capable people work on distinct projects, all active protagonists. Older Marlon in black cape carries another pair of chairs toward a crowded shared table; his red pencil is behind his ear. A playful practical ending that opens toward the team formation story. No motivational posters, extra slogans, fortress-of-solitude sign, or suggestion the Lab is outside Bok.

Caption: “In 2015, he founded the Learning Lab inside the Bok Center.”

Dialogue: Marlon, seeing the busy room: “We're going to need more chairs.”

Lettering: Outer arch: “BOK CENTER”. Inner doorway: “LEARNING LAB”.
