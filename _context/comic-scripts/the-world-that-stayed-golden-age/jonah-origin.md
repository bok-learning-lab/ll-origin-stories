# The World That Stayed: Golden Age

## Visual direction

Two portrait pages, each exactly four panels in a 2×2 grid, printed on yellowed newsprint. Draw this as an authentic 1938–1946 Golden Age comic: crude, energetic pen-and-brush inking, thick uniform black outlines, minimal feathering, simple stocky anatomy, square jaws, small dot eyes, stiff dynamic poses. Four-color letterpress: coarse Ben-Day dots in every tint, color plates slightly misregistered, ink spread, paper grain. Flat primaries only: red, blue, yellow, flesh, dull green, brown; gray built from dots. No gradients, glow, airbrush or modern rendering. Period design: brick seminar halls with art-deco trim, streamlined furniture, 1940s tweed and ties. Lettering: hand-drawn ALL CAPS in an uneven period hand; every Caption in a YELLOW rectangular box with a black border; dialogue in white balloons with pointed tails to the correct speaker; generous room for full sentences; letter only the quoted text; drafts carry unlettered scribble marks. Jonah is drawn from the attached sheet: fair skin, short slightly wavy brown hair lighter at the temples, ROUND BLACK GLASSES always on, calm dry wit. Civilian: brown tweed jacket over a mustard-yellow sweater vest and tie, dark trousers. Hero costume from the sheet: blue tights, yellow trunks, yellow boots, short yellow cape, white chest disc with a bracket mark, glasses on. No mask, no exaggerated muscles. A heroic teacher, never a camera operator. The academic planet sits under an enormous RED sun and remains weakened but inhabited and intact. The Bok Center is another world under an enormous YELLOW sun, containing a Learning Lab workshop within it. Portals between worlds are drawn as simple ringed doorways with radiating lines, pulp-science style. Scene metaphors are literal comic physics; institutional facts in captions are real.

## Panel 1 — Home

Art: Wide establishing view of the living academic planet under a huge red sun, its curved horizon crowned by brick seminar halls with art-deco trim. Foreground: Jonah in tweed and round glasses enters a classroom with a satchel and a stack of drafts. Other 1940s-dressed teachers and adult students are present. He is central, calm, attentive.

Caption: "In 2009, Jonah arrived in Expos: Harvard's writing program."

Caption: "On this world, teaching writing was everyday work."

## Panel 2 — What the work looked like

Art: Jonah and an adult student at a seminar table. The student's draft floats as interlinked sheets joined by red threads. Jonah points to a pivotal connection while the student actively moves a page into place. An elegant tangle begins to form a comprehensible structure. No readable words on the drafts.

Caption: "They read closely, followed arguments, and helped writers see what their ideas could become."

Dialogue: Jonah: "What does this example let you argue?"

## Panel 3 — Still there

Art: Panoramic view: the same red-sun planet visibly cracked, its brick buildings propped with timber and patched, yet classroom windows glow and people still teach inside. Jonah calmly closes a classroom door with drafts under his arm while a few harmless pebbles float in low gravity. No explosion; no evacuation.

Caption: "The world grew more precarious. It did not disappear."

Dialogue: Jonah: "We still have class."

## Panel 4 — Two appointments

Art: Full-body Jonah stands at a ringed portal doorway: red-sun Expos seminar on the left, yellow-lit Bok Center workshop on the right. One hand carries a draft portfolio; his yellow cape catches the yellow light. A threshold, not a permanent escape. Two caption boxes, clearly placed top and bottom.

Caption: "Jonah never left Expos. His appointment now spans two worlds."

Caption: "Half teaching in Expos. Half at the Bok Center, as assistant director for writing pedagogy."

## Panel 5 — Across the threshold

Art: Establish the Bok Center as a lively sunlit academic workshop with an open inner room, the Learning Lab, full of adult collaborators in 1940s clothes, paper models, books and pinned idea boards. Jonah arrives in his blue-and-yellow costume; the draft portfolio is the same one. No filming, microphones or cameras.

Caption: "At the Bok Center, he works closely with the Learning Lab."

Caption: "The craft he brings is writing pedagogy: helping people teach writing."

## Panel 6 — Same craft, another room

Art: Jonah works alongside two adult teachers at a large table. Floating assignment sheets are connected by yellow threads; he helps one teacher reposition a key instruction and the other points toward the resulting path. Action is shared; no mind control or magical superiority.

Caption: "Here, too, an assignment is a structure someone else has to find a way through."

Dialogue: Jonah: "What will students learn by doing this?"

## Panel 7 — Return trip

Art: Jonah steps back through the ringed portal toward the red-sun seminar world with the same satchel and portfolio. He is unmistakably returning, not fleeing. In the foreground a Bok colleague in a 1940s dress watches with friendly surprise; the still-standing planet is visible through the portal.

Dialogue: colleague: "You're going back?"

Dialogue: Jonah: "I teach there."

## Panel 8 — The ongoing arrangement

Art: Expansive last panel. Jonah stands poised mid-threshold with one boot on each world, red and yellow light split across his glasses and costume, drawn as two flat color fields meeting at a line. Both populated worlds are visible, alive. A few loose pages try to drift in opposite directions; he catches them with a small amused smile. Confident, slightly absurd, unresolved. No tragic isolation.

Caption: "The home world is still standing. So is the split appointment."

Dialogue: Jonah: "Same craft. Two calendars."
