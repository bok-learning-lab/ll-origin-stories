# The World That Stayed

## Visual direction

Idealized dramatic graphic superhero comic, confident black brush inks, readable faces, subtle print halftone, limited rich navy/mustard/crimson/cream palette. Jonah is recognizably based on his character-sheet image: fair skin, short slightly wavy brown hair graying at temples, round black glasses, light stubble, calm dry wit. Earth clothes navy checked overshirt and mustard tee. Hero outfit refined navy long jacket with mustard lining over dark clothing, glasses always on. No giant muscles, no mask. A heroic teacher, never a camera operator. Keep all images drawn, not photorealistic. Each page exactly four substantial panels in a 2 by 2 grid with cream gutters. Rectangular cream narration boxes with large black lettering establish facts. White speech balloons have clear tails. Generous lettering space; only supplied text appears. No cover/title page. Scene metaphors are literal comic physics; institutional facts in captions are real. An academic planet under a red sun has brick seminar halls, floating drafts and scribbled argument structures. It remains weakened but inhabited and intact. The Bok Center is another world under warm yellow light, containing a Learning Lab workshop within it. Space portals connect the worlds.

## Panel 1 — Home

Art: Wide establishing view of the living academic planet under a red sun, its curved horizon crowned by brick seminar halls. Foreground Jonah enters a classroom with a bag and a stack of drafts. Other teachers and students are present. He is central, calm, attentive.

Caption: "In 2009, Jonah arrived in Expos: Harvard's writing program."

Caption: "On this world, teaching writing was everyday work."

## Panel 2 — What the work looked like

Art: Jonah and an adult student at a seminar table. The student's draft floats as interlinked sheets with red connecting threads. Jonah points to a pivotal connection, while the student actively moves a page into place. An elegant tangle begins to form a comprehensible structure. No readable incidental words on drafts.

Caption: "They read closely, followed arguments, and helped writers see what their ideas could become."

Dialogue: Jonah: "What does this example let you argue?"

## Panel 3 — Still there

Art: Panoramic view: the same red-sun planet visibly cracked, its buildings propped and patched, yet classroom windows glow and people still teach inside. Jonah calmly closes a classroom door with drafts under his arm while a few harmless pebbles drift in low gravity. No planet explosion; no evacuation.

Caption: "The world grew more precarious. It did not disappear."

Dialogue: Jonah: "We still have class."

## Panel 4 — Two appointments

Art: Full-body Jonah stands at an open portal, red-sun Expos seminar on the left, warmly lit Bok Center workshops on the right. One hand carries a draft portfolio; his jacket lining catches the yellow light. A visual threshold, not a permanent escape. Two caption rectangles, clearly placed top and bottom.

Caption: "Jonah never left Expos. His appointment now spans two worlds."

Caption: "Half teaching in Expos. Half at the Bok Center, as assistant director for writing pedagogy."

## Panel 5 — Across the threshold

Art: Establish the Bok Center as a lively sunlit academic workshop with an open inner room: the Learning Lab, full of adult collaborators, paper models, books and idea boards. Jonah arrives in his subtly heroic navy long jacket; the draft portfolio is the same one. No filming, microphones or cameras.

Caption: "At the Bok Center, he works closely with the Learning Lab."

Caption: "The craft he brings is writing pedagogy: helping people teach writing."

## Panel 6 — Same craft, another room

Art: Jonah works alongside two adult teachers at a large table. Floating assignment sheets are connected by luminous mustard threads; he helps one teacher reposition a key instruction and the other points toward the resulting path. Action is shared, no mind control or magical superiority.

Caption: "Here, too, an assignment is a structure someone else has to find a way through."

Dialogue: Jonah: "What will students learn by doing this?"

## Panel 7 — Return trip

Art: Jonah steps back through the portal toward the red-sun seminar world with the same bag and portfolio. He is unmistakably returning, not fleeing. In the foreground a Bok colleague watches with friendly surprise; the still-standing planet is visible through the portal.

Dialogue: colleague: "You're going back?"

Dialogue: Jonah: "I teach there."

## Panel 8 — The ongoing arrangement

Art: Expansive last panel. Jonah stands poised mid-threshold with one shoe on each world, red and yellow light mixing across his glasses and navy coat. Both populated worlds are visible, alive. A few loose pages try to drift in opposite directions; he catches them with a small amused smile. Confident, slightly absurd, unresolved. No tragic isolation.

Caption: "The home world is still standing. So is the split appointment."

Dialogue: Jonah: "Same craft. Two calendars."

## Editorial notes

Script written directly by the agent, not by a separate text-generation API. Factual anchor: origin-stories-briefing.md, Jonah Johnson entry; corrections in the user thread govern. Jonah arrived in Expos in 2009, never left, has a half Expos / half Bok appointment, works closely with the Lab, and does not do video. His named superpower remains open, so this script shows writing pedagogy without claiming a settled new power formulation. Portal, levitating pages, red/yellow light, dialogue, classmates/colleagues, and costume are fictional staging. No Marlon cameo, preserving Jonah's point of view. No student identities or real assignments used.
