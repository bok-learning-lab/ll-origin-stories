Authentic Golden Age American comic book, 1938 to 1946, reproduced as a printed page from a cheap newsstand comic: yellowed newsprint with visible paper grain, foxing and worn edges; four-color letterpress printing with coarse visible Ben-Day dots inside every tint; color plates slightly misregistered so flat color sits a hair outside the black line; ink spread. Drawing in the manner of the earliest Action Comics, Detective Comics and Whiz Comics: thick uniform black pen-and-brush outlines, almost no feathering or crosshatching, simple stocky heroic anatomy, square jaws, small dot-and-dash eyes, stiff dynamic poses, big simple hands. Strictly flat limited palette: red, blue, yellow, flesh tint, dull green, brown, and gray built from dots. No gradients, no glow effects, no airbrush, no digital lighting, no lens flare, no modern crosshatch rendering, no manga, no photorealism. Period design vocabulary: finned rocket ships, art-deco towers, streamlined machines with dials and radio tubes, 1940s suits, hats and dresses, newsreel cameras. Hand-ruled slightly wobbly panel borders with cream paper gutters. Hand-drawn ALL-CAPS lettering in an uneven period comic hand; captions in YELLOW rectangular boxes with a black border; speech balloons white with a pointed tail to the speaker.
