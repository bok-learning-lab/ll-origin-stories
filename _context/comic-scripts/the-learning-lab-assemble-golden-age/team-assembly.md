# The Learning Lab Assemble: Golden Age

## Visual direction

Two portrait pages, each exactly four panels in a 2×2 grid, printed on yellowed newsprint. Draw this as an authentic 1940–1946 Golden Age team comic in the manner of the earliest Justice Society stories: crude, energetic pen-and-brush inking, thick uniform black outlines, minimal feathering, simple stocky heroic anatomy, square jaws, small dot eyes, stiff dynamic poses, big simple hands. Four-color letterpress: coarse Ben-Day dots in every tint, color plates slightly misregistered outside the black line, ink spread, paper grain, worn corners. Flat primaries only: red, blue, yellow, flesh, dull green, brown; gray built from dots. No gradients, glow, airbrush or modern rendering. Period design: art-deco hall with a round meeting table, streamlined machines with dials and radio tubes, 1940s clothes on supporting figures. Lettering: hand-drawn ALL CAPS in an uneven period hand; every Caption in a YELLOW rectangular box with a black border; dialogue in white balloons with unmistakable pointed tails; letter only the quoted text. FOUR distinct real people from the FOUR attached sheets; preserve each identity and costume. MARLON: bald, thick dark eyebrows, beard flecked white, blue tights, red trunks and boots, yellow belt, red cape, yellow chest shield with a red pencil; holds a red pencil. JORDAN: long dark hair, blue-green suit with red chevron, yellow belt, bracelets and headband, dark riding boots; ribbons of banded color. MADELEINE: long strawberry-blonde hair with bangs, forest-green tunic with yellow fern emblem, short yellow cape, yellow boots, silver pendant; thick tabbed grimoire. JONAH: short wavy brown hair, ROUND BLACK GLASSES, blue tights, yellow trunks, boots and short cape, white chest disc with bracket mark; draft portfolio; NO camera or video role. They are equal colleagues with different powers. Fictional comic architecture; institutionally the Learning Lab is INSIDE the Bok Center. Narration distinguishes the 2015 founding from the gathering over later years. Supporting figures are anonymous adults. The final panel must show all four together, distinct and equally visible, in heroic team formation.

## Panel 1 — A place to build

Art: Wide scene-setting view of the Bok Center, an art-deco building under a huge yellow sun. A cutaway reveals a new open workshop within it: long tables, books, tools, blank walls waiting for projects. Marlon in costume unrolls a floor plan with his red pencil, looking toward the enormous room. Inside and outside are one institution. The origin of a shared base, not a solitary cave.

Caption: “In 2015, Marlon founded the Learning Lab inside Harvard's Bok Center.”

Caption: “The idea was a shared workshop for experiments in teaching, learning, and making.”

Lettering: Outer building: “BOK CENTER”. Inner entrance: “LEARNING LAB”.

## Panel 2 — The invitation

Art: Time has passed. Marlon at the same now-busy table opens a pile of academic projects that rise into a constellation of disconnected pages, pictures and diagrams. He draws a red connecting line outward like a beacon; three ringed doorways with radiating lines open around the room, anticipating collaborators from different worlds. No other named hero yet. The complexity is exciting, not a threat.

Caption: “Over the years, people arrived with different histories—and different powers.”

Caption: “The work needed more than one way of seeing.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “Bring what you know.”

## Panel 3 — Jordan brings the color

Art: Jordan enters through a ringed doorway opening onto her lush island, blue surf and horses visible outside. Her blue-green costume, red chevron and long dark hair match her sheet. She sweeps a banded ribbon of color across gray prototypes, revealing bright shapes people can pick up and try. A capable artist-educator, not a decorative presence.

Caption: “Jordan brought arts education—and the power to make a room come alive with color and possibility.”

Dialogue: Jordan, holding a vivid prototype: “Let's try it.”

## Panel 4 — Madeleine brings the grimoire

Art: Madeleine arrives through a book-shaped doorway with fern pages and folklore figures behind her. She opens her tabbed grimoire; its branching yellow symbols become a small working machine of boxes and tubes on the shared table. Her face, strawberry-blonde hair, bangs, green tunic and yellow cape match her sheet.

Caption: “Madeleine brought folklore, catalogs, and spellcraft: ways to build imaginative systems that carry meaning.”

Dialogue: Madeleine: “The instructions are in the book.”

## Panel 5 — Jonah keeps the passage open

Art: Jonah steps through a red-sun ringed doorway from the still-inhabited writing-program world. Through it people are teaching in a brick seminar, never ruins. He wears his blue-and-yellow costume and round black glasses and carries a draft portfolio. Leave the door visibly OPEN behind him. Marlon recognizes his home world with a warm surprised look, but Jonah is the focal figure.

Caption: “Jonah never left Expos. His appointment spans the writing program and the Bok Center.”

Caption: “He brings writing pedagogy—and keeps the passage between worlds open.”

Dialogue: Jonah: “We still have class back there.”

## Panel 6 — One project, four approaches

Art: All four gather around ONE central round table carrying an enormous unfinished academic project: archival pictures, scattered research pages and a blank presentation board. Clearly separate their contributions: Marlon reveals red conceptual connections, Jordan makes a colorful paper prototype, Madeleine's grimoire sends out yellow branching paths, Jonah arranges prompt sheets that give learners a clear way in. Anonymous collaborators listen and decide together. Four faces identifiable.

Caption: “At the same table, a tangle of research became a shared experiment.”

Caption: “Connections. Color. Imagination. A way for someone else to learn.”

## Panel 7 — The doors open

Art: The resulting installation is now a working, colorful exhibition with pinned pages, pictures and tabletop models. Adult participants in 1940s clothes move between them pursuing different questions. All four heroes are embedded in the room helping people use what they built, each in their distinct costume: Marlon holds a red pencil, Jordan hands over a model, Madeleine opens a branching display, Jonah discusses a question with a participant. The result is concrete and usable.

Caption: “Now other people could enter the project, test an idea, and make something of their own.”

Dialogue: A participant: “Can we try another version?”

## Panel 8 — The league, assembled

Art: A full-team hero portrait as the final panel. Low-angle group shot in the grand open entrance of the Learning Lab with the busy workshop and yellow light behind them. All FOUR appear together, three-quarter to full length, equal visual weight: Jordan at left with a color ribbon and brush; Marlon center-left, bald with flecked beard, red cape, holding the red pencil; Madeleine center-right with grimoire, strawberry-blonde hair and yellow cape; Jonah at right with round black glasses, yellow cape and draft portfolio. No fifth hero, no duplicated faces, none obscured. An ensemble of distinct abilities, not a leader and sidekicks. Expressions mix confidence and dry humor. Reserve the upper margin for the caption and a modest doorway sign.

Caption: “The Learning Lab. Different home worlds. One shared headquarters.”

Lettering: Doorway: “LEARNING LAB”.
