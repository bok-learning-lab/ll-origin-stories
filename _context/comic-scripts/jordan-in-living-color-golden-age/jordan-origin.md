# Jordan in Living Color: Golden Age

## Visual direction

Two portrait pages, each exactly four panels in a 2×2 grid, printed on yellowed newsprint. Draw this as an authentic 1941–1946 Golden Age comic in the manner of the early adventure heroines: crude, energetic pen-and-brush inking, thick uniform black outlines, minimal feathering, simple stocky anatomy, small dot eyes, stiff dynamic poses. Four-color letterpress: coarse Ben-Day dots in every tint, color plates slightly misregistered, ink spread, paper grain. Flat primaries only: red, blue, yellow, flesh, dull green, brown; gray built from dots. No gradients, glow, airbrush or modern rendering. The story's gag is printed color itself: island panels use every color plate at full strength; the Harvard FAS panels are printed almost only in black ink and gray dots with a single small color accent; the Learning Lab panels are back in full four-color. Lettering: hand-drawn ALL CAPS in an uneven period hand; every Caption in a YELLOW rectangular box with a black border; dialogue in white balloons with pointed tails to the correct speaker; generous room for full sentences; letter only the quoted text. Jordan is drawn from the attached sheet: long dark-brown hair, expressive arched brows, long oval face, strong nose, warm wry smile. On her ancient island she wears an ivory linen riding tunic with dark riding trousers and boots; her modern civilian look is a 1940s ivory blouse with dark trousers; her hero costume from the sheet is a blue-green suit with a red chevron across the chest, yellow belt, yellow bracelets, yellow headband, and dark riding boots; practical, not sexualized. Hair stays long and dark. She rides a bay horse. The home island is ancient, magical, lush and extravagantly colorful: weathered stone courtyards, pots of pigment, orchards, tilled fields, horses, dramatic mountains beside blue surf; never a modern campus. Every island inhabitant is on horseback, including artists at easels. Avoid stereotyped island cultural clothing; this is a fictional arts-and-horses fantasy, not a representation of Hawaiian culture.

## Panel 1 — A place where color belongs

Art: Grand establishing view of an ancient magical island: green mountains, blue surf, terraced farmland, orchards, grazing horses, weathered stone towers and open courtyards bright with painted murals. Jordan rides a bay horse in the foreground in her ivory riding tunic and dark trousers, long hair blowing. Every inhabitant is a mounted artist painting murals or passing pots of pigment. Flat painted motifs float above the stone on radiating lines. No modern school, logos or campus.

Caption: "Jordan came from an ancient island where everyone was an artist."

Caption: "The fields were fertile, the courtyards full of color—and everyone rode horses."

## Panel 2 — The home world's ordinary magic

Art: Medium shot of Jordan on her horse painting a brilliant mural on an ancient courtyard wall beside a mounted artist friend. Beyond them: farmland with horses, green terraces, mountains and crashing blue surf. Jordan casually releases a ribbon of living color from her brush, drawn as a flat banded rainbow stripe; her friend is unsurprised. Magic is ordinary here.

Caption: "Imagine the seas and mountains of Hawaii, with farmland and horses like rural Georgia."

Caption: "Here, making things colorful was simply part of making things."

## Panel 3 — Another atmosphere

Art: Jordan steps from the brilliant outdoors into a dim, bland Harvard academic corridor of gray stone, dark wood doors and a bare hanging lamp, printed almost entirely in black ink and gray dots. Her ivory blouse and one small colorful handmade paper model at her waist are the only color. Wide doorway transition shot. No horse indoors.

Caption: "Then Jordan entered another world: Harvard's Faculty of Arts and Sciences."

Caption: "The light was dimmer. So was the palette."

## Panel 4 — A curious local expression

Art: At a drab seminar table, printed in black and gray dots, Jordan offers the bright handmade paper model. An anonymous gray academic in a gray 1940s suit and spectacles studies it with a tight patronizing smile. Jordan raises one eyebrow, amused rather than crushed. Color belongs only to her model and one tiny accent.

Caption: "She soon learned that enthusiasm had a local dialect."

Dialogue: Gray academic: "Oh, that's fun."

Caption: "It did not sound like a compliment."

## Panel 5 — The power made visible

Art: Dramatic low angle. Jordan now wears her blue-green costume with red chevron, yellow belt, bracelets and headband. In one broad gesture she sweeps a banded ribbon of color across the gray seminar room; wherever it passes, full four-color printing returns and turns into murals, paper shapes and bright tools people can use. No destruction, no enemies knocked down. Heroic expression wry and determined, with period radiating action lines.

Caption: "Jordan's power was adding color—and making room for people to create."

Dialogue: Jordan: "Let's try it."

## Panel 6 — Participation

Art: The same seminar table transformed into a busy shared making session in full color. Adult students in 1940s clothes build paper structures and draw vivid marks; gray sleeves become colored as people reach for materials. Jordan helps a participant assemble a model, practical and hands-on, not magically controlling anyone.

Caption: "Color spread through the room as people picked up materials and joined in."

Dialogue: Participant: "Can we make another one?"

## Panel 7 — A room already in color

Art: Jordan stands in open double doors looking into a bright creative workshop in full four-color, wearing her costume. Inside: worktables, colored stage lamps, art supplies, a newsreel camera, sketches, and cheerful unnamed collaborators. Wide welcoming composition. No portraits of other named staff; no extra hero identities.

Caption: "At the Learning Lab, inside the Bok Center, Jordan found a room already in full color."

Dialogue: Lab colleague: "What are you making?"

## Panel 8 — Same words, new meaning

Art: Warm humorous final panel. In the Lab, Jordan and a colleague admire an elaborate brightly colored tabletop miniature of the island with tiny horses and a surf wave. Jordan keeps the same face and costume and an amused raised eyebrow. The colleague beams. Miniature horses echo page one; no solemn pose.

Dialogue: Lab colleague: "Oh, that's fun!"

Dialogue: Jordan: "Now you're speaking my language."

## Editorial notes

Golden Age edition, 2026-09-11. Captions and dialogue are carried over verbatim from the approved script in _context/comic-scripts/jordan-in-living-color/jordan-origin.md; the visual direction and art staging were rewritten by the agent for an authentic 1940s printed-comic look, using the four-color printing process itself to stage the color gag. Jordan is from Georgia, not Atlanta; the island is a mythic place described through comparisons to Hawaii and rural Georgia, with horses. The costume, magic, anonymous seminar exchange, participatory transformation and tabletop island are fictional staging. No separate text API was used.
