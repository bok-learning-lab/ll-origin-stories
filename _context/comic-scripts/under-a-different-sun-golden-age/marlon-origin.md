# Under a Different Sun: Golden Age

## Visual direction

Two portrait pages, each exactly four panels in a 2×2 grid, printed on yellowed newsprint. Draw this as an authentic 1938–1941 Golden Age superhero comic in the manner of the earliest Superman stories: crude, energetic pen-and-brush inking, thick uniform black outlines, minimal feathering, simple stocky anatomy, square jaw, small dot eyes, stiff dynamic poses, big simple hands. Four-color letterpress printing: coarse Ben-Day dots in every tint, color plates slightly misregistered outside the black line, ink spread, paper grain, worn corners. Flat primaries only: red, blue, yellow, flesh, dull green, brown; gray made of dots. No gradients, glow, airbrush, digital lighting or modern rendering. Period design: finned rocket ships, art-deco domes and towers, streamlined machines with dials and radio tubes, 1940s shirts, ties, cardigans and hats. Lettering: hand-drawn ALL CAPS in an uneven period hand; every Caption in a YELLOW rectangular box with a black border; dialogue in white balloons with pointed tails to the correct speaker; leave generous room for full sentences; letter only the quoted text; papers and books carry unlettered scribble marks, never extra words. This is ONE Marlon at two ages, drawn from the attached sheets. Panels 1–4: younger Marlon, bald, thick dark eyebrows, dark beard, civilian black shirt with sleeves rolled and dark trousers. Panels 5–8: older Marlon, bald, beard flecked white, arriving in the same civilian clothes, then wearing the sheet's costume: blue tights, red trunks, red boots, yellow belt, red cape, yellow chest shield bearing a red pencil; never a cowl or mask. The age shift is fictional time compression. The red pencil is the visual form of pattern recognition; red lines connect ideas. Before escape: a writing-program planet under an enormous RED sun. After: the funded world under an enormous YELLOW sun. Camp cosmic peril, sincere account of the work. Fictional building design. No claim he was hired for video, no camera as an escape ticket, no video-reel comparison.

## Panel 1 — The writing planet

Art: Wide view inside a seminar room on a rocky alien planet beneath an enormous red sun that fills the window. Younger Marlon and several differently drawn writing teachers in 1940s shirtsleeves, ties and cardigans sit around a long table stacked with drafts. Everyone casually holds up and rearranges parts of a magnificent floating structure of manuscript pages and conceptual diagrams. Art-deco domes and craggy alien rock beyond the glass. Marlon is one among capable colleagues, not yet exceptional.

Caption: “Marlon came from Expos, Harvard's writing program.”

Caption: “On that planet, they taught writing. They studied how ideas fit together.”

## Panel 2 — Ordinary powers

Art: Closer view of younger Marlon working alongside a colleague and a visiting researcher. Several floating clusters contain distinct unlettered visual systems: a mathematical diagram, a historical map, a literary manuscript. He notices the shared structure and draws a bold red line joining two ideas; the researcher begins rearranging their own pages into a coherent whole. Show actual pattern recognition across academic work, not grading errors or correcting punctuation. His face, pencil gesture, and the coherent arrangement must read clearly.

Caption: “They could enter an unfamiliar academic world, spot its patterns, and help someone turn a tangle of notes into work that made sense.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “These two ideas belong together.”

## Panel 3 — The ground moves

Art: The same seminar tilts as jagged cracks spread through the ground outside and chunks of rock lift into the red sky. Teachers keep working together with the floating pages; one hand steadies a teacup. A finned retro rocket stands on a distant launch gantry. Camp cosmic peril in the 1930s pulp manner, no casualties. The world is threatened, not conclusively destroyed.

Caption: “To its inhabitants, none of this was a superpower. It was the work.”

Caption: “But the ground beneath that world was never quite secure.”

## Panel 4 — What he carried

Art: Younger Marlon strapped into the cockpit of a finned rocket with big dials and a round porthole, surrounded by annotated drafts, notebooks and books. He still connects pages with his red pencil as stars streak past the window as speed lines. Through the porthole a brilliant red flare half hides the home planet; its survival remains possible. No camera prop, hiring scene, ticket, rescuer, or stated reason for departure. The luggage is intellectual habits.

Caption: “When Marlon left, he took the habits of that world with him.”

## Panel 5 — A funded sun

Art: Older Marlon, beard flecked white, climbs down the rocket's ladder in his black shirt and dark trousers. The Bok Center is an art-deco building under an enormous yellow sun. Welcoming collaborators in 1940s clothes carry manuscripts, a newsreel camera on a tripod, and reels of punched tape from a room-sized electronic brain with dials and glowing tubes. Academic diagrams are pinned everywhere. No camera handoff, recruitment speech, or focus on why he was hired. The yellow light falls on abundant working materials.

Caption: “In 2010, he landed at the Bok Center, in a world of code, cameras, and academic projects.”

Caption: “This world had a different sun: funding.”

Lettering: Building name: “BOK CENTER”.

## Panel 6 — A familiar problem

Art: A collaborator stands with a mass of research notes, diagrams and photographs floating over a worktable. A perfectly functional newsreel camera and a console with dials stand nearby. Older Marlon, now in his blue costume with red cape, studies the material with the same attentive head tilt and pencil gesture as panel 2. The collaborator is intellectually engaged, not helpless.

Caption: “The tools were different. The questions were familiar.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “Let's find the structure.”

## Panel 7 — Under a different sun

Art: Older Marlon's red pencil connects the floating ideas. They snap into a legible structure of argument, pictures and machine symbols; collaborators actively take pieces and develop their own projects. Strong graphic echo of panel 2: same ability, changed context. Marlon is surprised by their surprise, not smug. Caped figure and the bold red connecting line give the heroic scale, with period radiating action lines.

Caption: “Here, his old abilities became superpowers: recognizing patterns, learning new systems, and helping other people's ideas take shape.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “We did this all the time back home.”

## Panel 8 — Room for a team

Art: Wide view into a bustling, colorful collaborative hall visibly INSIDE the Bok Center. An outer arch says Bok Center, an inner doorway says Learning Lab. Several capable people in 1940s clothes work on distinct projects at long tables, all active. Older Marlon in his red cape carries another pair of wooden chairs toward a crowded shared table; the red pencil is behind his ear. A playful practical ending that opens toward the team story. No motivational posters, extra slogans, or suggestion the Lab is outside Bok.

Caption: “In 2015, he founded the Learning Lab inside the Bok Center.”

Dialogue: Marlon, seeing the busy room: “We're going to need more chairs.”

Lettering: Outer arch: “BOK CENTER”. Inner doorway: “LEARNING LAB”.
