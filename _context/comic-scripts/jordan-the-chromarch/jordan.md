# Jordan: The Chromarch

## Visual direction

1980s corporate-gothic supervillain comic. Hand-drawn expressive ink with dense black masses, coarse printed halftones, aged cream paper, acid magenta, emerald, crimson and cobalt. Angular architecture, gothic research rooms and sinister corporate patronage. LANDSCAPE 3:2 page containing exactly FOUR panels in a 2x2 grid, equal quadrants, thick black borders and cream gutters. Large legible cream rectangular narration boxes and white speech balloons. Match villain ensemble sheet faces and final costumes. No photographic rendering, modern smartphones, brand logos or extra lettering. Speaker labels are instructions, not printed words. Each page must tell a visible story, with varied camera distance and concrete action. The protagonist starts in the plain clothes specified in panels 1–4, with the SAME face and hair as the reference. The full reference villain costume appears when specified, firmly by panels 5–8. Only this protagonist from the ensemble appears. Supporting characters must be distinct anonymous adults. All wrongdoing, foundations, supernatural objects and origins are fictional. No invented real hiring motives or trauma.

## Panel 1 — The island’s argument

Art: Mythical island of artists, vivid sea and mountains, farmland and horses. Jordan long dark hair, simple teal riding clothes, rides past three radically different colorful murals. Artists cheerfully disagree while everyone has paintbrushes. Palette has genuinely varied imperfect combinations.

Caption: “Jordan’s mythical home had seas and mountains like Hawaii, farms and horses like rural Georgia. Everyone made art. Nobody agreed.”

Dialogue: Jordan: “That was the best part.”

## Panel 2 — The colorless commission

Art: A gray-clad civic foundation board receives Jordan’s vivid drawings in a sterile black-marble chamber. Outside its huge windows an entire neighborhood has been rendered gray. She remains in simple teal clothing with riding boots.

Caption: “A private foundation asked her to revive a district it had made perfectly respectable.”

Dialogue: Official: “Something inspiring. Within reason.”

## Panel 3 — The instrument

Art: In a vault under an old art school, Jordan finds the color-wheel scepter from her villain sheet. Its beam turns a blank architectural model into many competing vivid possibilities. White horse waits by the vault stair, safe and curious.

Caption: “Beneath the school lay a prism that made an imagined color physically real.”

Dialogue: Jordan: “Why stop at the walls?”

## Panel 4 — The opening

Art: Spectacular Jordan on her white horse with scepter high: dull district blooms into vivid murals, textiles and public gardens. Adult residents take brushes and contribute different colors themselves. She wears emerging violet-teal cape, expression genuinely joyful.

Caption: “Her first act gave the street back to its artists.”

Dialogue: Jordan: “Make something I haven’t thought of.”

## Panel 5 — The unexpected answer

Art: An adult artist with cropped dark hair and orange work overalls paints a small stark black-and-white rectangle amidst surrounding vivid murals. Jordan, now in full villain costume, pulls up her white horse beside it, visibly affronted. Artist looks satisfied.

Caption: “One artist took her at her word.”

Dialogue: Artist: “Mine needs some quiet.”

## Panel 6 — The correction

Art: Jordan deliberately lowers her prismatic scepter at the black-and-white artwork. Vivid identical teal and violet bands replace it; the artist’s black paintbrush also turns teal. Show her choice clearly, not an accidental power discharge.

Caption: “She could leave room for that difference. Instead, she corrected it.”

Dialogue: Jordan: “You’re confusing freedom with poor judgment.”

## Panel 7 — A beautiful order

Art: THE CHROMARCH rides above the transformed district. Everyone now wears the same violet-teal cape and repeats the same picture at identical easels; prismatic rays visibly hold their arms in matching poses. Exuberant colors, unmistakably constrained people.

Caption: “As THE CHROMARCH, she made every citizen an artist. The same artist.”

Dialogue: Jordan: “Look how confident everyone’s become.”

## Panel 8 — Curatorial discretion

Art: Close comic reveal: Jordan’s white horse leaves one muddy hoofprint on the pristine violet-teal palace floor. A uniformed attendant bends with a cloth; Jordan stops them with scepter. The spontaneous brown mark sits behind a newly placed velvet rope.

Caption: “Only her horse was still allowed to make an unapproved mark.”

Dialogue: Jordan: “Leave it. He has an instinct for earth tones.”

## Editorial notes

Agent-authored exact script, written before generation. Biographical skills come from the current project brief; patrons, crises, artifacts, choices, actions, dialogue and malicious traits are alternate-universe inventions. The eight-panel structure establishes a useful skill, grants a literal power, shows a beneficial first use, introduces an independent person or living process, then depicts the protagonist deliberately suppressing it. The final image preserves an unresolved flaw and a dry comic beat. Supporting characters are anonymous adults, not other workshop participants.
