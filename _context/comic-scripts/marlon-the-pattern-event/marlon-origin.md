# Marlon: The Pattern Event

## Visual direction

Classic 1960s Jack Kirby/Fantastic Four-era drawing, bombastic cosmic machinery, powerful blocky heroic anatomy, huge expressive hands, crisp black brush masses, flat cyan/red/yellow, Ben-Day dots and black cosmic crackle. Not photorealistic. Use supplied CLASSIC Marlon sheet: bald, thick eyebrows, short gray-flecked beard, black T-shirt/jeans before accident, black/navy suit with wide silver piping and silver belt/cape afterward. Red pencil as focus of literal structural powers. Portrait page of exactly four panels in 2×2 grid; pale-yellow rectangular exposition boxes, white clear speech balloons. Exact quoted lettering only. This is an alternate comic universe: an invented cosmic-ray exposure creates the supernatural power; prior writing-teacher skill supplies its form. Not the immigrant/planet-escape story. No invented video hiring motive. Learning Lab inside Bok Center. No deaths or injuries depicted.

## Panel 1 — Before the ray

Art: Marlon and colleagues teaching writing in an Expos seminar, a table of ordinary academic manuscripts. His red pencil circles a connection on a student's draft; nothing yet levitates. Expressive human-scale establishing scene.

Caption: “Before the accident, Marlon taught writing in Expos, Harvard's writing program.”

Caption: “He read for patterns: how ideas fit together, and what a complicated project was trying to become.”

## Panel 2 — A machine for seeing

Art: Later at Learning Lab inside Bok, Marlon in civilian black studies a towering absurd 1960s cosmic-ray scanner with magnetic rings, vacuum tubes, banks of giant dials and a document tray. Researchers place heterogeneous academic diagrams in the tray. This is plainly extravagant comic-fiction machinery, not a real historical apparatus.

Caption: “At the Learning Lab, an experimental scanner promised to reveal hidden structures in academic work.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “What could possibly be hard about that?”

## Panel 3 — The exposure

Art: The machine erupts in cosmic radiation and Kirby crackle; one jagged brilliant red-white ray strikes Marlon and the red pencil in his hand. Dramatic foreshortened palm, flying pages, saturated yellow blast. His body outlined in impossible geometry. No gore.

Caption: “Then the scanner met a pattern it could not contain.”

Lettering: “KRAK!”

## Panel 4 — The invisible becomes visible

Art: Staggering but unharmed Marlon sees solid glowing red structural lattices connecting every page, object and diagram across the room. He raises a hand and a red line physically pulls two distant fragments together. This is newly acquired supernatural ability, not metaphor alone.

Caption: “The exposure changed him. Connections were no longer things he merely understood.”

Caption: “He could see them—and move them.”

## Panel 5 — The pattern storm

Art: Marlon now in classic black/silver hero suit confronts the scanner expanding into an immense vortex that tears pictures, equations and manuscript pages into incompatible layers. Collaborators shelter at consoles, intellectually engaged. Huge cosmic machinery and energy arcs.

Caption: “The scanner kept running. Its broken pattern was beginning to pull the whole workshop apart.”

## Panel 6 — An old habit, a new force

Art: Marlon flies or lunges into the vortex, gigantic foreshortened red-pencil hand foreground. He recognizes one shared geometric form across a map, argument and diagram, connecting them into an enormous stable red framework. Cosmic hero action with precise conceptual logic.

Caption: “Years of entering unfamiliar intellectual worlds gave him somewhere to start.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “These belong together!”

## Panel 7 — Something people can use

Art: The red framework becomes a stable working structure; the machine powers down as collaborators actively arrange its different components into a clear interactive academic project. Marlon guides rather than controls them. Big satisfying reversal, white/yellow energy against blue shadows.

Caption: “He gave the pieces a structure. The people around him made it work.”

## Panel 8 — The next draft

Art: The Lab restored, Marlon in hero suit quietly holds a tiny whiteboard while a huge luminous diagram still floats overhead. Colleagues eye the inadequate board. Playful confident cosmic-comic final pose, not a moral speech.

Caption: “The Learning Lab, inside the Bok Center. Now with one rather unusual writing teacher.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “We're going to need a bigger whiteboard.”

## Editorial notes

Assistant-authored alternate-universe fiction based on Marlon's writing-teaching and pattern-recognition account. The cosmic scanner, exposure, danger and dialogue are invented. The event creates literal supernatural ability, unlike the previous elsewhere-origin or the innate mutant version. No actual radiation incident or historical device is asserted. Exact script precedes image generation.
