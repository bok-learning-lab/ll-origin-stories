# Jordan: Color Outlaw — Extended

## Visual direction

Dark gritty mutant superhero comic, 1990s-and-later edgy hand-inked graphic noir. Dense black shadow masses, scratchy drybrush, aggressive crosshatching, dramatic foreshortening and rain-slick oppressive academic stone. Near-monochrome charcoal/cold blue at Harvard, cut by fierce teal/coral/gold chromatic light. The home island is richly colored but still drawn in the same heavy ink. Not photorealistic or glossy airbrushed movie art. Retain Jordan's recognizable long dark-brown hair, long oval face, strong nose, arched brows and wry mouth from the supplied dark character sheet. Costume throughout: practical black riding jacket with small teal/coral seam accents, black trousers, black riding boots, gold wrist cuffs. No cape, mask or sexualized design. In home-island panels the same black outfit reads as fantasy riding gear, not a different costume. Four equal panels per page in 2x2 reading order. Cream rectangular expository boxes with large black lettering; white speech bubbles. No additional panels, titles or words. This mutant power is innate from birth; NO radiation, experiment or accident causes it. Suppression of difference is fictional genre staging, not an allegation of real personal abuse. No blood or weapons. Homeworld is an ancient magical artist island: sea/mountains compared to our world's Hawaii, farms/horses to rural Georgia, no literal combined campus or actual ancient biography.

## Panel 1 — Her first world

Art: Wide ancient island at dramatic dusk, mountains and turquoise surf, cultivated farmland, horses, stone courts covered in art. Jordan in black riding clothes sits on her bay horse among mounted painters. Rich color with deep expressive ink shadows, visibly ancient rather than a modern campus.

Caption: "Jordan came from an ancient island where everyone was an artist."

Caption: "Picture Hawaii's sea and mountains, with farmland and horses like rural Georgia."

## Panel 2 — An ordinary difference

Art: Medium view in old stone artist courtyard. Jordan casually opens her empty hand and a small living colored horse appears over it without a brush. A fellow artist on horseback continues painting nearby, unsurprised. Serious beautiful twilight and wry warm face, innate magic normal here.

Caption: "She was born with a mutant gift: the power to bring color into the world."

Caption: "At home, difference was ordinary."

## Panel 3 — The approved atmosphere

Art: Heroic low angle Jordan walks through a rain-dark academic stone canyon, nearly all black/cold gray. Severe repeating windows and distant gray silhouettes. Only faint teal/coral seams distinguish her from shadows; recognizable face and raised chin. No guns, no pursuit, no trauma.

Caption: "Then she entered Harvard's Faculty of Arts and Sciences."

Caption: "Here, the world seemed determined to keep its colors to itself."

## Panel 4 — Keep it small

Art: At gray seminar table Jordan presents a colorful folded-paper horse. Her fingers glow faintly. On the right, a gray academic leans on one hand with patronizing tight smile. Jordan on left looks at him with skeptical raised eyebrow. Bubble tail clearly points only to academic.

Caption: "She learned that even enthusiasm could be a way to put something in its place."

Dialogue: Gray academic: "Oh, that's fun."

## Panel 5 — Inside the lines

Art: A severe institutional gray drafting board encloses every idea in identical rigid ruled boxes. Jordan stands beside it, a tiny bright shape in her hand pressing against a box edge. Gray anonymous official on right points at the grid; Jordan on left. Powerful shadow metaphors, no physical restraint. Single official bubble clear.

Caption: "In this world, even imagination came with an approved outline."

Dialogue: Gray official: "Stay inside the lines."

## Panel 6 — Difference made visible

Art: Jordan opens both hands in massive foreshortened heroic pose. Innate teal/coral/gold light erupts from her palms through the gray grid, becoming colored paper, marks and sculptural shapes people can use. People step forward to make things together; no mind control. Heavy black ink against electriccolor, fierce defiance without injury.

Caption: "The harder this world pressed for sameness, the brighter her difference became."

Dialogue: Jordan: "I'm not keeping it small."

## Panel 7 — The room inside

Art: Jordan pauses at open studio doors in black riding jacket; beyond them is a shadowy creative haven with stage lights, brushes, drawings, cameras and worktables lit by saturated colors. An unnamed colleague gestures her inside. Keep this dark noir, not brightly clean sitcom. Both faces distinguishable; one speaker.

Caption: "At the Learning Lab, inside the Bok Center, she found room to make something strange."

Dialogue: Colleague: "Bring it in."

## Panel 8 — Bigger

Art: Jordan on right and a friendly distinct colleague on left work on a miniature island sculpture. Luminous horses race among farmlands, cliffs and breaking surf; the miniature threatens to expand beyond the table. Both faces lit by fierce color from below with mischievous smiles. Still scratchy noir shadows. Correct speaker tails and reading order, no extra dialogue.

Dialogue: Colleague: "Oh, that's fun!"

Dialogue: Jordan: "Let's make it bigger."

## Editorial notes

Expanded from the four-panel Color Outlaw variant to eight panels at Marlon's request. Exact captions/dialogue authored before generation. The existing dark character sheet is reused without regenerating it. The added room establishes the ancient artist island, innate gift, new gray setting, pressure to conform, and arrival at the Lab. The power is explicitly innate; no technological exposure creates it. This is deliberate comic fiction and does not claim a real incident, diagnosis or abuse. Georgia correction controls. The original four-panel project remains preserved.
