# The Learning Lab: Assemble

## Visual direction

An exuberant, dramatic graphic superhero comic: bold confident black inks, expressive simplified faces, strong silhouettes, rich flat colors, cinematic warm/cool light, restrained halftones. Two portrait pages, each four equal panels in a 2×2 grid with ivory gutters. Rectangular pale-yellow narration boxes carry scene setting; dialogue in white balloons with unmistakable speaker tails. Render only explicitly quoted lettering. Four distinct real people, represented by the FOUR attached character sheets; preserve their identities and their separate costume palettes. Marlon: bald, dark eyebrows, silver-gray beard, black superhero suit/cape, red pencil. Jordan: long dark hair, teal/coral/gold heroic riding outfit, color ribbons. Madeleine: strawberry-blonde hair/bangs, black clothes and plum botanical cloak, tabbed grimoire. Jonah: short wavy brown/graying hair, round black glasses, navy heroic long coat with mustard/gold lining, draft portfolio; NO camera/video role. They are equal colleagues with different powers. The building is fictional comic architecture but institutionally the Learning Lab is INSIDE the Bok Center. Narration distinguishes 2015 founding from the group gathering over later years. No claim that the four arrived together in 2015. Supporting figures are anonymous adult collaborators. Fantasy staging is an invented illustrative assembly, not a documentary event. Final panel must show all four together, distinct and equally visible, in heroic team formation.

## Panel 1 — A place to build

Art: Wide scene-setting view of the Bok Center in warm golden light. Architectural cutaway reveals a new, open workshop within it: broad tables, books, tools, walls waiting for projects. Marlon in black hero attire unrolls a floor plan with his red pencil, looking toward the enormous room. Inside and outside are one institution, not competing buildings. This is the origin of a shared base, not a solitary cave.

Caption: “In 2015, Marlon founded the Learning Lab inside Harvard's Bok Center.”

Caption: “The idea was a shared workshop for experiments in teaching, learning, and making.”

Lettering: Outer building: “BOK CENTER”. Inner entrance: “LEARNING LAB”.

## Panel 2 — The invitation

Art: Time has passed. Marlon at the same now-busy table opens a pile of academic projects that rise into a constellation of disconnected pages, pictures and diagrams. He draws a red connecting line outward like a beacon; three doorway-like openings glow around the room, anticipating collaborators arriving from different worlds. No other named hero yet. The complexity is exciting rather than a threat to be defeated.

Caption: “Over the years, people arrived with different histories—and different powers.”

Caption: “The work needed more than one way of seeing.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “Bring what you know.”

## Panel 3 — Jordan brings the color

Art: Jordan enters through a doorway opening onto her lush imaginary island, turquoise surf and horses visible outside. Her teal/coral/gold riding-hero outfit and long dark hair match her sheet. She sweeps a ribbon of color across gray prototypes, revealing playful shapes people can actually pick up and try. She is a capable artist-educator, not a decorative presence.

Caption: “Jordan brought arts education—and the power to make a room come alive with color and possibility.”

Dialogue: Jordan, holding a vivid prototype: “Let's try it.”

## Panel 4 — Madeleine brings the grimoire

Art: Madeleine arrives through a book-shaped doorway with botanical pages and folklore imagery behind her. She opens a tabbed grimoire; its branching gold symbols become a small working interactive system on the shared table. Her face, strawberry-blonde hair, bangs, black clothes and plum botanical cloak match sheet. No actual medieval biography asserted.

Caption: “Madeleine brought folklore, catalogs, and spellcraft: ways to build imaginative systems that carry meaning.”

Dialogue: Madeleine: “The instructions are in the book.”

## Panel 5 — Jonah keeps the passage open

Art: Jonah steps through a red-sun doorway from the still-inhabited writing-program world. Through it people are working in a seminar, never ruins. He wears his navy-and-mustard heroic coat, round black glasses and carries a draft portfolio. Leave the door visibly OPEN behind him. Marlon recognizes a familiar home world with a surprised warm look, but Jonah is focal figure and actor.

Caption: “Jonah never left Expos. His appointment spans the writing program and the Bok Center.”

Caption: “He brings writing pedagogy—and keeps the passage between worlds open.”

Dialogue: Jonah: “We still have class back there.”

## Panel 6 — One project, four approaches

Art: All four gather around ONE central worktable carrying an enormous unfinished academic project: archival pictures, scattered research pages and a blank presentation surface. Clearly separate their contributions: Marlon reveals red conceptual connections, Jordan makes a colorful tangible prototype, Madeleine's grimoire creates branching ways to explore it, Jonah arranges prompts that give learners a clear way into the work. Collaborators listen and make decisions together; no one merely watches the leader do everything. Four faces identifiable.

Caption: “At the same table, a tangle of research became a shared experiment.”

Caption: “Connections. Color. Imagination. A way for someone else to learn.”

## Panel 7 — The doors open

Art: The resulting installation is now a working, colorful interactive exhibition. Adult participants move between pages, images and tangible models, pursuing different questions. All four heroes are embedded in the room, helping people use what they built, each preserving their distinct costume. Marlon holds a red pencil, Jordan hands over a model, Madeleine opens a branching interface, Jonah discusses an interpretive question with a participant. The combined result is concrete and usable, not just a glowing orb.

Caption: “Now other people could enter the project, test an idea, and make something of their own.”

Dialogue: A participant: “Can we try another version?”

## Panel 8 — The league, assembled

Art: A full-team hero portrait as the final panel. Low-angle cinematic group shot in the grand open entrance of the Learning Lab, with the busy workshop and warm golden light behind them. All FOUR appear together, full or three-quarter length, equal visual weight: Jordan at left with a color ribbon/brush; Marlon center-left bald/gray beard in black cape holding red pencil; Madeleine center-right with grimoire, strawberry-blonde hair and plum cloak; Jonah at right with round black glasses, navy coat with mustard lining and draft portfolio. No fifth hero and no duplicated faces. None obscured by another or a caption. An ensemble of distinct abilities, not Marlon and sidekicks. Their expressions mix confidence and dry humor. Reserve upper margin for caption and modest doorway sign.

Caption: “The Learning Lab. Different home worlds. One shared headquarters.”

Lettering: Doorway: “LEARNING LAB”.

## Editorial notes

Text authored by the assistant from the user-supplied staff briefing, subsequent corrections, and the explicit request for a founding/Justice-League-like assembly arc ending in a group shot. Founding date and institutional relationships are factual; the invitation, arrivals through portals, the example shared research exhibition, every line of dialogue, and costumes are fictional staging. Membership is not presented as simultaneous hiring or original 2015 roster. Jonah remains split Expos/Bok and works with the Lab; no video role. Madeleine's coding is meaningful imaginative advantage, not catch-up or a claim other disciplines are inferior. Jordan contributes creative possibility, not merely cosmetic color. Marlon's power remains conceptual structure and pattern recognition, not a hiring-for-video account. Existing character sheets are reused without new generation.
