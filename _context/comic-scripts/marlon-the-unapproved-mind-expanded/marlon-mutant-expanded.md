# Marlon: The Unapproved Mind — Expanded

## Visual direction

Extremely dark gritty modern mutant comic, 1990s-and-later graphic novel. Oppressive impossible architecture, rain, harsh perspectives, scratchy black brushwork, dense crosshatching, desaturated blue-black and dirty bone with violent RED connection lines. All people idealized hand-drawn comic characters, never photorealistic. Actual input is approved dark MARLON sheet: bald head, broad dark eyebrows, recognizable nose, gray-stubbled beard, black t-shirt and jeans, battered charcoal longcoat/half-cape, black layers and heavy boots, RED PENCIL in hand. Preserve his actual face. Exactly four panels in a clear portrait2x2 grid per page, black borders, clean pale gutters. Pale bone RECTANGULAR captions and white dialogue balloons, all words large readable and exact. Letter only quoted text; names after Dialogue specify speaker, do not letter names. Fictional authoritarian city demands an approved outline for every thought; no claim that Harvard or a real institution commits crimes. Mutation is born-in and emerges under pressure, not caused by rays, money, tools or accident. Intense psychological burden is alternate-universe fiction, no real trauma/diagnosis claim. No gore/firearms. All collaborators adults. No camera/video or hiring premise. Vary closeup, broad city, kinetic power, intimate helping scene; grim ending without cure or cheering.

## Panel 1 — The work

Art: Rain-black metropolis outside a seminar room; foreground bald gray-bearded Marlon in black t-shirt reads several messy drafts with adult writers. Different disciplines represented by diagrams, historical images and equations on papers, no readable incidental text. Faint red connections glimmer invisibly to everyone except him.

Caption: "Marlon taught in Expos, Harvard's writing program. He helped people turn complicated ideas into work that made sense."

Caption: "The subjects changed. His eye for patterns carried across them."

## Panel 2 — Keep it in the margins

Art: The imagined city's enormous geometric grid towers above a tiny writing desk. Marlon's face in shadow, a red pencil held still over a margin where connections strain toward other pages. He presses his free hand over the glow, trying to keep it contained. Black architecture, no actual institution logo.

Caption: "In this darker city, every mind was expected to follow an approved outline."

Caption: "For years, he kept the connections in the margins."

## Panel 3 — The pressure

Art: An adult project maker tries to fit a huge spread of notes into a narrow rigid diagram. Important images and pages are left outside, visibly disconnected. Marlon watches the unfinished work buckle against the frame. The frame is a literal impossible metaphoric object, no torture or weapons.

Caption: "Then a project met an outline too small to hold it. The essential connection was being cut away."

Dialogue: collaborator: "It fits. But it isn't what I mean."

## Panel 4 — The difference surfaces

Art: Marlon reaches out; red networks erupt from his hands and eyes, across pages, walls and ceiling. His red pencil traces a huge connection outside the approved diagram. He looks shocked at the uncontrolled scale, not triumphant. Foreground collaborator sees their pages reconnect. No external beam or laboratory apparatus.

Caption: "Under pressure, the difference surfaced. It had been inside him all along."

Caption: "Now the hidden relationships became visible—and he could change their shape."

## Panel 5 — No off switch

Art: Lonely longcoat Marlon in narrow rainwashed alley. Red structural lines pierce every wall, window, puddle and cloud, overwhelming geometries extending beyond the frame. Close face exhausted and alert, one hand over his eyes but red lines remain in vision. No physical injury, no implication of real diagnosis.

Caption: "He could not switch it off. Every wall became a system; every loose thread demanded attention."

## Panel 6 — Start where it breaks

Art: At a dim warm workshop table Marlon bends beside an adult collaborator with an unfinished project: research notes, a physical model, several images, a draft. He uses the RED PENCIL to illuminate a connection between two pieces; the collaborator physically rearranges them. An intimate helping gesture, concrete progress, no mind control.

Caption: "But a connection could do more than haunt him. It could help someone find the shape of an unfinished idea."

Dialogue: Marlon: "Show me where it stops making sense."

## Panel 7 — A place to unfold

Art: Wider establishing view of the Learning Lab workshop inside the Bok Center, a warm refuge within the fictional dark city. Marlon holds a branching red network steady overhead; three anonymous adult collaborators actively develop distinct projects below, each choosing their own structure. Diverse work remains different, no cloning one diagram. Outside, imposed black grid persists.

Caption: "At the Bok Center's Learning Lab, different minds could work without becoming the same."

Dialogue: Marlon: "Keep the part that doesn't fit."

## Panel 8 — Hold the opening

Art: Final hard dramatic image Marlon in battered black longcoat cuts a red structural doorway through a huge black imposed grid using his pencil. Behind him people continue their own work in the warm workshop. He faces the rain and the still-dark city, grim, determined, neither cured nor alone. The door remains physically open. No cheering or idealized utopian skyline.

Caption: "The city had not changed its outline. He kept an opening in it."

Dialogue: Marlon: "Leave the door open."

Lettering: Doorway: "LEARNING LAB".
