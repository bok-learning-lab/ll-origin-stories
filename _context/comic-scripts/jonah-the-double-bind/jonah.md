# Jonah: The Double Bind

## Visual direction

1980s corporate-gothic supervillain comic. Hand-drawn expressive ink with dense black masses, coarse printed halftones, aged cream paper, acid magenta, emerald, crimson and cobalt. Angular architecture, gothic research rooms and sinister corporate patronage. LANDSCAPE 3:2 page containing exactly FOUR panels in a 2x2 grid, equal quadrants, thick black borders and cream gutters. Large legible cream rectangular narration boxes and white speech balloons. Match villain ensemble sheet faces and final costumes. No photographic rendering, modern smartphones, brand logos or extra lettering. Speaker labels are instructions, not printed words. Each page must tell a visible story, with varied camera distance and concrete action. The protagonist starts in the plain clothes specified in panels 1–4, with the SAME face and hair as the reference. The full reference villain costume appears when specified, firmly by panels 5–8. Only this protagonist from the ensemble appears. Supporting characters must be distinct anonymous adults. All wrongdoing, foundations, supernatural objects and origins are fictional. No invented real hiring motives or trauma.

## Panel 1 — Between worlds

Art: Jonah with wavy brown hair and glasses, ordinary navy jacket rather than split villain suit, carries two annotated manuscripts through an open doorway between two writing seminar rooms. Keep both rooms active and inhabited, not destroyed.

Caption: “Jonah’s work spans Expos and the Bok Center. This fictional origin begins with the skill that serves both: understanding an argument.”

Dialogue: Jonah: “What would persuade the other person?”

## Panel 2 — The disputed passage

Art: Two anonymous institution directors on opposite sides of a grand hall with a chained shared library door. Frustrated adult readers wait. Jonah stands between directors with a small notebook. One director is a bald clean-shaven man, the other an older woman with short white hair.

Caption: “Two private institutes hired him to settle who could use their shared library.”

Dialogue: Director: “Our door must remain open. Their people must remain outside.”

## Panel 3 — The loophole in the glass

Art: In deserted library at night, Jonah opens a narrow silver case containing two facing hand mirrors and a ribbon-like strip of paper. Reflections show him wearing the final split navy/ivory suit, while the actual man is still dressed plainly.

Caption: “In the archive he found a pair of mirrors that could make an agreed premise physically binding.”

Dialogue: Jonah: “That would save a great deal of time.”

## Panel 4 — The successful argument

Art: Jonah holds the two mirrors toward the two directors, now together in front of the library door. Their spoken agreement turns into ivory ribbons lifting the chain from the door. Readers behind them look relieved. Use one dialogue balloon shared between the directors.

Caption: “His first bargain opened the library.”

Dialogue: Directors: “Access serves us both.”

## Panel 5 — The refusal

Art: One adult reader, short curly hair and a red scarf, stands at a table covered in a long entry contract. Jonah now wears the split suit and two watches, mirrors on desk, and offers a pen. She calmly pushes the pen back.

Caption: “The next visitor declined his terms.”

Dialogue: Reader: “I’d like to read the book without joining either side.”

## Panel 6 — The new premise

Art: Close-up: Jonah deliberately tightens an ivory ribbon around the two mirrors. The reader’s handwritten empty signature space folds itself into a doorway. She watches in alarm. Jonah’s face is composed, not enraged.

Caption: “He could accept her refusal. Instead, he defined it as participation.”

Dialogue: Jonah: “Declining the agreement confirms that you received it.”

## Panel 7 — Both entrances

Art: THE DOUBLE BIND in full reference costume stands between two spectacular ivory and navy doorways. Ivory ribbons visibly route both entrance paths into ONE central transparent cage holding adult visitors. Jonah remains outside, holding ribbon ends like a master puppeteer.

Caption: “As THE DOUBLE BIND, he made every available choice lead to the same commitment.”

Dialogue: Jonah: “Your alternatives have been carefully preserved.”

## Panel 8 — The opt-out

Art: The red-scarfed reader sits silently on a chair inside the transparent cage, arms folded, refusing even to look at the proffered form. Jonah, outside, annotates her entirely blank response sheet with a gold pen; the untouched exit door is behind him.

Caption: “One visitor chose silence.”

Dialogue: Jonah: “Excellent. No objections.”

## Editorial notes

Agent-authored exact script, written before generation. Biographical skills come from the current project brief; patrons, crises, artifacts, choices, actions, dialogue and malicious traits are alternate-universe inventions. The eight-panel structure establishes a useful skill, grants a literal power, shows a beneficial first use, introduces an independent person or living process, then depicts the protagonist deliberately suppressing it. The final image preserves an unresolved flaw and a dry comic beat. Supporting characters are anonymous adults, not other workshop participants.
