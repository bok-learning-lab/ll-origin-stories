# Jonah: Between the Lines — Expanded

## Visual direction

Very dark gritty adult mutant graphic novel, 1990s-and-later noir: heavy dry-brush black ink, scratchy crosshatching, rain-slashed scenes, bruised indigo and dirty gray with sparse toxic lime light, sharp cinematic angles, oppressive monumental architecture, long silhouettes and intense close-ups. Entirely drawn, never photorealistic. Jonah's actual attached sheet fixes his likeness: round black glasses, short wavy brown hair gray at temples, long oval face, light stubble. Modern mutant outfit: long distressed charcoal hoodless coat with asymmetrical fastenings, ragged hem and slender lime inner seam, layered dark gray shirt, black pants, heavy boots; no badge/mask/cape. Four panels in a clear 2x2 grid on each tall portrait page. Large off-white rectangular caption boxes with crisp black lettering. White dialogue balloons and tails clearly attributed. No speaker labels lettered. Only specified quoted text appears. An expressly fictional mutant alternate universe; no claim of real trauma, diagnoses, persecution, or institutional crimes. Born with latent mutation; pressure reveals it. No radiation, exposure or laboratory accident causes it. No gore. All learners and colleagues adults. No cameras, video, money or wealth motifs.

## Panel 1 — Under the page

Art: Rainy night at a seminar room. Jonah studies a draft beneath a small pool of lamplight; outside rise enormous city buildings made of unreadable clauses. Thin lime joints inside the architecture glimmer in his black glasses. Ordinary adult classmates work around him, unaware of the superimposed structures.

Caption: "In Expos, Harvard's writing program, Jonah taught writing. In this darker world, he had always seen something underneath it."

## Panel 2 — Latent

Art: Closeup Jonah's eye through round glasses, reflected draft lines turning into a half-seen staircase. His hand pauses over a margin. No beam striking him, no external cause. The gift has always been inside him. Tight claustrophobic composition.

Caption: "The mutation was his from birth. For years, it had looked like an unusually strong sense of where an argument led."

Dialogue: Jonah: "There's a turn here."

## Panel 3 — Pressure

Art: An adult learner sits at a table, a dense assignment sheet above them unfolding into towering black walls. Jonah stands nearby, small beneath the architecture. The learner faces several blank closed doorways; walls are metaphorical pressure made physical, not a prison or crime.

Caption: "Then an assignment became a maze—and its writer could no longer see a way through."

Dialogue: learner: "I don't know where to start."

## Panel 4 — Emergence

Art: Dramatic foreshortened hand Jonah thrusts into a wall of language. Acid-lime structural lines blaze through the dark, uncovering a luminous door-shaped seam. Glasses reflect fierce geometry. Learner sees it too; they are no longer trapped alone in confusion.

Caption: "Under pressure, the hidden structure blazed into view. He could expose its doors—and move them."

Dialogue: Jonah: "There. That's a way in."

## Panel 5 — Both places

Art: Establish rainy street with two distinct lit thresholds: red Expos seminar world and cool Bok workshop world. Jonah crosses between them carrying a draft portfolio. His charcoal coat glows faint lime, both inhabited rooms visible. No abandoned home, no evacuation.

Caption: "He never left Expos. His appointment is half teaching there, half writing pedagogy at the Bok Center."

Caption: "The same craft. Another room."

## Panel 6 — The cost of seeing

Art: Jonah pauses alone in the rainy street. The hidden clause-architecture overlays EVERYTHING—windows, traffic reflection, puddles and clouds. His hand briefly touches the bridge of his glasses but the structures persist. Tired and resolute, no injury or diagnosis. Darkness presses close.

Caption: "But the sight would not switch off. Every sentence became architecture."

Caption: "He could not stop seeing the walls."

## Panel 7 — A choice for someone else

Art: Inside a dim Bok workshop, Jonah holds apart one tall wall of paper, revealing three branching lime passageways. An adult teacher and adult learner study their choices; the learner points decisively toward one route. Jonah keeps the opening clear rather than choosing their destination. No speech from anyone except Jonah.

Caption: "Working with teachers and the Learning Lab, he learned to make openings without choosing everyone's route."

Dialogue: Jonah: "Which way lets you say what you mean?"

## Panel 8 — An ongoing power

Art: Final wide cinematic frame Jonah braces a huge page-wall with both hands, charcoal coat battered by rain, black glasses catching lime. One learner steps through an opening toward a branching city while another approaches. The surrounding walls persist, but passage is real. Defiant half-smile, no triumphant cure or tidy acceptance.

Caption: "The walls remained. So did the possibility of another way through."

Dialogue: Jonah: "A wall is not the only way to read this."
