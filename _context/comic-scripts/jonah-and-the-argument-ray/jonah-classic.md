# Jonah and the Argument Ray

## Visual direction

Vivid authentic 1960s cosmic superhero comic. Jack Kirby / early Fantastic Four dynamism: thick angular black ink, huge foreshortened hands, black cosmic crackle, impossible diagram-machines, flat cyan/magenta/yellow/navy colors, Ben-Day dots on warm newsprint. NOT photographic, NOT modern digital painting. Jonah's actual reference sheet fixes his round black glasses, short wavy brown-and-gray hair, long face and light stubble. Civilian checked navy overshirt/mustard tee. Post-accident superhero bright cobalt fitted suit, saffron chest and shoulder blocks, white circular chest badge with two black square brackets, saffron gloves and boots, no cape or mask, glasses retained. Four panels in a clear 2x2 grid on a portrait page, bold black borders and ivory gutters. Large readable cream rectangular narration boxes; dialogue balloons separate with correct speaker tails. Only quoted text is lettered; speaker names are instructions. Metaphor is literal alternate-universe fiction, not biographical reconstruction. All students/colleagues are adults. No cameras, video or filming. No deaths or bodily injury.

## Panel 1 — The ordinary craft

Art: Cosmic wide view of Expos, a brick-built academic planet under a red sun. Cutaway classroom in foreground shows Jonah with adult writers, rearranging draft pages at a seminar table. Ordinary clothes. This world's teachers all work with arguments; no superpower yet.

Caption: "On the world of Expos, Harvard's writing program, Jonah taught people to make arguments."

Caption: "At first, he did it the ordinary way."

## Panel 2 — Extraordinary apparatus

Art: In a fantastical seminar archive, Jonah turns the crank of an outrageous cyclotron-sized mimeograph. Giant printer rollers, glowing vacuum tubes and paper feed arms create cosmic geometry. A colleague watches. This is humorous impossible machinery, clearly not a real Harvard lab.

Caption: "Then came the experimental Argument Engine. It was supposed to duplicate seminar handouts."

Dialogue: Jonah: "How hard can a second copy be?"

## Panel 3 — The accident

Art: The machine bursts into an immense magenta/cyan beam filled with paper silhouettes and black crackle. Jonah is thrown back in foreshortening, glasses still on, engulfed in radiant abstract diagram energy. No gore or injury. Machine physically transforms into geometric light.

Caption: "A jam in the impossible machine released a beam of pure argument."

Lettering: "KRAK!"

## Panel 4 — New sight

Art: Close dramatic face Jonah reflected in glasses. Now he physically sees bright bridges between floating claims and evidence, with gaps like deep space. Gesture extrudes one tangible bridge from his hand. His ordinary clothes acquire cobalt/saffron geometric superhero shapes through the energy.

Caption: "The exposure changed him. Connections between ideas became visible—and solid."

Dialogue: Jonah: "That paragraph is doing all the work!"

## Panel 5 — A useful power

Art: Full heroic cobalt/saffron Jonah braces a huge floating argument bridge while an adult writer chooses where to place the next evidence-shaped block. A dazzling gap closes only as the writer acts. Jonah helps, not mind-controls.

Caption: "He could reveal a missing connection. The writer still had to decide what to say."

Dialogue: Jonah: "What does your evidence change?"

## Panel 6 — Another room

Art: Establish Bok Center under yellow sun as a shining academic city with its Learning Lab workshop inside. Jonah and adult teachers work together around a large floating assignment maze, turning a blocked corridor into several navigable routes. Same superhero costume and glasses.

Caption: "At the Bok Center, he worked with teachers and the Learning Lab: making routes for other people to learn."

Dialogue: Jonah: "Where can a student begin?"

## Panel 7 — Still on both worlds

Art: A monumental portal frames both red Expos and yellow Bok. Jonah holds it open as pages stream between two populated classrooms. Expos remains patched but alive. He stands with one boot on each side, competent rather than fleeing.

Caption: "He never left Expos. Half his appointment is teaching there; half is writing pedagogy at Bok."

## Panel 8 — A familiar problem

Art: Comic cosmic finale! Jonah in magnificent saffron/cobalt suit confronts a tiny jammed desktop duplicator. His hands emit immense geometric power, yet one wrinkled sheet remains stuck. A stack of handouts waits. Glasses, deadpan raised eyebrow. No threatening institution or humiliation.

Caption: "Two worlds. One extraordinary craft."

Dialogue: Jonah: "The second copy is still stuck."

## Editorial notes

Commissioned genre reinterpretation. Agent authored exact script before image generation. The Argument Engine, accident, beam, powers, costume and dialogue are explicitly alternate-universe fiction, not biographical events. Actual anchor: Jonah arrived in Expos in 2009, never left, and holds half Expos / half Bok appointment as assistant director for writing pedagogy, working closely with the Learning Lab. No video. This classic version creates powers through an extraordinary exposure, unlike the mutant version.
