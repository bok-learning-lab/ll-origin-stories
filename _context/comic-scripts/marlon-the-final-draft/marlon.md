# Marlon: The Final Draft

## Visual direction

1980s corporate-gothic supervillain comic. Hand-drawn expressive ink with dense black masses, coarse printed halftones, aged cream paper, acid magenta, emerald, crimson and cobalt. Angular architecture, gothic research rooms and sinister corporate patronage. LANDSCAPE 3:2 page containing exactly FOUR panels in a 2x2 grid, equal quadrants, thick black borders and cream gutters. Large legible cream rectangular narration boxes and white speech balloons. Match villain ensemble sheet faces and final costumes. No photographic rendering, modern smartphones, brand logos or extra lettering. Speaker labels are instructions, not printed words. Each page must tell a visible story, with varied camera distance and concrete action. The protagonist starts in the plain clothes specified in panels 1–4, with the SAME face and hair as the reference. The full reference villain costume appears when specified, firmly by panels 5–8. Only this protagonist from the ensemble appears. Supporting characters must be distinct anonymous adults. All wrongdoing, foundations, supernatural objects and origins are fictional. No invented real hiring motives or trauma.

## Panel 1 — Other people’s ideas

Art: Marlon, bald with gray beard, in a plain dark cardigan at a writing-teaching table. Anonymous adult writer opposite him has a tangled manuscript. His ordinary red pencil circles a meaningful connection; loose pages become readable visual shapes, not actual extra text.

Caption: “Before this fictional fall, Marlon helped writers find patterns in their own ideas.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “There’s an argument here. Let’s find yours.”

## Panel 2 — A patron of clarity

Art: An anonymous silver-haired foundation patron in a severe gray suit shows Marlon a huge city redevelopment model buried under transparent layers of conflicting proposals. Marlon is still in cardigan, holding pencil. Rain outside black marble boardroom.

Caption: “A private foundation offered him a laboratory, unlimited funding, and an impossible commission.”

Dialogue: Patron: “Make all of this make sense.”

## Panel 3 — The machine

Art: Wide underground laboratory. Marlon works alone at a colossal red-pencil mechanism ringed with manuscript rollers and glass lenses. From its tip a crimson line links miniature streets to the real city visible beyond. A black cape with red lining hangs unused nearby.

Caption: “He built an editorial engine. It could revise the world as readily as a sentence.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “First, remove the unnecessary obstacles.”

## Panel 4 — The welcome revision

Art: Close dramatic view: his red pencil crosses a drawn locked gate in an architectural plan, while in the same frame the real gate outside dissolves into red proof marks. Ordinary people begin crossing a formerly blocked public bridge.

Caption: “His first revision opened a bridge that competing authorities had kept closed.”

Dialogue: Citizen: “You made a way through!”

## Panel 5 — A second author

Art: A writing-room collaborator, an adult woman with short silver hair and round earrings, offers a vivid branching alternative plan. Marlon now wears the reference black/crimson costume, next to the engine. She points to homes omitted from the simplified model.

Caption: “Then someone found a pattern his plan had missed.”

Dialogue: Collaborator: “These people need a different ending.”

## Panel 6 — The decisive edit

Art: Close-up of Marlon’s gloved hand drawing one enormous red deletion line through the collaborator’s branching proposal. Behind the translucent page her open mouth releases an empty speech balloon. Do not print any words in that empty balloon. His face shows cold deliberate choice.

Caption: “He could revise his design. Instead, he revised her objection.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “It weakens the throughline.”

## Panel 7 — The final draft

Art: Marlon fully transformed into THE FINAL DRAFT, giant pencil scepter and crimson-lined cape, stands over a city now forced into one straight red avenue. Citizens’ many paths have become a single file. The collaborator walks silently among them, still holding her branching manuscript.

Caption: “As THE FINAL DRAFT, he gave every life one direction. His.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “No one gets lost anymore.”

## Panel 8 — No further notes

Art: Intimate villain office. Marlon signs a monumental bound city plan. From a wastebasket, a tiny blank unlined page curls upward like a new leaf. He traps it beneath an enormous glass paperweight, calm and pleased.

Caption: “The city was coherent. Unfortunately, tomorrow still had ideas.”

Dialogue: Marlon: “We’ll discontinue the blank pages.”

## Editorial notes

Agent-authored exact script, written before generation. Biographical skills come from the current project brief; patrons, crises, artifacts, choices, actions, dialogue and malicious traits are alternate-universe inventions. The eight-panel structure establishes a useful skill, grants a literal power, shows a beneficial first use, introduces an independent person or living process, then depicts the protagonist deliberately suppressing it. The final image preserves an unresolved flaw and a dry comic beat. Supporting characters are anonymous adults, not other workshop participants.
