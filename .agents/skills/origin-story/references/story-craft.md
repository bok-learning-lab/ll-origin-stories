# From context to panels

Find the causal line: something ordinary or strange in one setting produces an ability that acts on another. Distinguish a transferable competency from the distinctive approach it makes possible. The new world should present a concrete situation in which the power can be seen working.

Four panels often hold place → formative event → manifestation → present use. Eight panels can give the old world's ordinary life room, show the crossing, delay recognition of the power, and land a visual callback. These are starting shapes, not a template into which every life must fit. A split appointment, for example, may call for a recurring commute rather than an irreversible crossing.

Each panel needs one drawable instant, purposeful framing, and legible lettering. Use rectangular narration boxes to establish names, institutions, background, abilities, elapsed time, and causal relationships when the reader needs them. Exposition is part of comic storytelling: a full clear sentence can do more than a cryptic fragment. Let dialogue give people a voice and let the artwork make the narration concrete; neither has to carry all the context alone. Vary scale: establishing view, intimate action, revelation, payoff. Reuse an object, shape, or gesture with changed meaning. Keep a joke inside the fictional world instead of adding an ironic narrator to excuse the whole enterprise.

Before image generation, read only the exact captions, dialogue, and labels, without the brief or art directions. Can a new reader identify the home world, understand what people there practiced, name the protagonist's actual abilities, and follow why those abilities matter in the new world? Check each causal assertion against the user's current account. Do not substitute a job title, hiring anecdote, or clever metaphor for the stated power. Rewrite language that requires the author to explain it aloud. Record the source of the text separately from the image renderer: agent-authored wording remains the agent's responsibility even when an image model renders it.

## Genre variations

| Frame | What makes the power work | What must change in the script |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elsewhere / DC-like origin | Abilities ordinary at home become extraordinary under new conditions | Show the home world's normality and the destination's different physics; don't default to destroying home |
| Exposure / 1960s cosmic-science origin | An extraordinary accident creates literal new powers, shaped by prior interests and practice | Establish ordinary life before the accident, depict the exposure clearly, then show a new physical capability solving a concrete problem; do not conflate this with an innate mutation |
| Mutant / X-Men-like origin | An unusual capacity already marks the person within their own world | Show the earlier misfit experience only when supported by the brief; make new invented scenes visibly fictional; a team provides company and an arena, not a cure |
| Tools / Batman–Iron Man-like origin | Resources, practice, infrastructure, and obsession build the capacity | Replace innate magic with equipment and labor; for the Lab parody, institutional purchasing is part of the joke, not an invented claim of personal wealth |

For comparative versions, say briefly what changed in the explanation of the power. Do not present these interpretive frames as a settled historical taxonomy. Research comics history separately if asked.

When the user requests strongly different visual editions, make new character sheets for each edition and use that edition's compressed JPEG sheets consistently across solo stories, team pages, and covers. Keep the earlier editions intact. Dark mutant fiction can push oppressive architecture, unstable powers, hard shadows, damaged clothing, and unresolved danger without inventing real biographical trauma. The team's role is solidarity and room to act, not curing the difference. An eight-panel exposure story and four-panel mutant reinterpretation are useful comparative defaults when the brief calls for that scope; team frames can still use eight panels.

A team-up needs a present-tense situation that requires distinct abilities to interact. Recruitment is not the same as creating the recruits' powers. Origins can be flashback inserts with clear return points. Marlon can convene without becoming the sole hero with three assistants. In the workshop examples, separate origins led to an assembly story and later genre reinterpretations. Treat that sequence as an example, and generate only the cast, lengths and installments currently requested.

## Script format for the bundled prompt preparer

Use a title, a `## Visual direction` section, then `## Panel 1 — …` through `## Panel N — …`. Inside each panel use paragraphs prefixed `Art:`, `Caption:`, `Dialogue:`, and/or `Lettering:`. Art directions must identify speakers and keep unlettered notes out of quoted dialogue. Specify exactly what text may appear. Put production decisions and questions in a final `## Editorial notes` section; the preparer excludes it.

The visual direction locks the chosen style, palette, character continuity, and reference-image roles. Do not invent age, race, hair, or clothing as biographical facts when appearance is unknown. Offer photos when likeness matters. If the user chooses to proceed without them, create a coherent fictionalized visual design and label that choice in the brief. Replace any old pending-likeness note with the resulting concrete character direction before generation; do not carry a request for more information into an image prompt.

Before delivery, read just the captions and dialogue aloud, then just the art. The first should sound like a person; the second should still tell the story. Verify names, dates, institutional relationships, and whether the last beat earns its space.


## Frame stories and page order

Develop the individual origin scripts first so the framing story can depend on their actual abilities. Then write a separate four- or eight-panel frame: an opening situation that requires a team and an assembly/payoff that makes the abilities interact. It may be read before and after the origins even though it was drafted afterward. An eight-panel frame provides an opening four-panel page and a closing four-panel page. Record every page in the production plan's `readingOrder`, using `story-id:page-number`. No page should disappear or repeat accidentally when the frame is split around the origins.
