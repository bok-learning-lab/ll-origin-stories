# Intake and optional interview

Start by reading the supplied text. Infer what is already answered and identify only the missing information that would materially affect the story. Offer a short follow-up: for example, “I can work from this. Would you like to add a moment when this ability first showed up, share appearance details or a photo, have a longer interview, or have me proceed?” Adapt the question to the gaps; do not ask that entire list when only one thing is missing.

“Just go with what you have,” “you decide,” “that's enough,” or an equivalent instruction ends information gathering. Work with the available material. Record important assumptions in the output brief and use invented stage business without passing it off as biography. Missing photos do not block a fictionalized comic; distinguish its invented visual design from an accurate portrait. If the user's first request already says to proceed without questions, do so immediately.

## Longer interview, only when requested

Ask one short question per turn. Start with “Where are you from?” only when the supplied text has not already answered it. Follow the answer without praise or a recap before every question. Move from place to experience to power to a scene in which it works. Follow interesting departures and accept “skip.”

Useful things to uncover: a particular room or landscape; an object; what was ordinary there; a move, job, encounter, or sustained practice; what the person can now do that is unusual around them; the first concrete evidence; what remains unresolved between their worlds. “I'm resilient” needs an action. A person need not have left home, suffered, or discovered a new identity to have a story.

At “that's enough,” stop asking. Write a brief separating supplied facts, the person's words for their ability, and unresolved details or drafting assumptions. Then proceed to the requested deliverable unless the user asked only for an interview or brief.

The original September 10 activity paired origins with “I Want” songs. Collect dissatisfaction and a future want only if the user also requests that song material; do not force it onto an origin comic.
